# 💔 How I Learned From Rejection in Open Source

This page isn’t a highlight reel—it’s the stuff I’d rather forget, but need to remember.  
Open source taught me more from pushback than praise.

---

## The First PR That Got Shot Down

I spent an entire weekend hacking on a feature for kubeflow. I was honestly proud:  
I finally opened the pull request… and got a _public_ list of 9 things I messed up.

I wanted to disappear. Then I realized:

- The reviewer cared enough to give specific, actionable feedback.
- Their questions made my code (and reasoning) better.
- Every “no” was a future “almost yes”.

---

## Top Lessons From Rejected PRs

1. **Read the Contribution Guidelines (twice!):**  
   My early PRs failed basic checks I could have avoided.

2. **Small PR > Big PR:**  
   Large, multi-feature PRs rarely land. Focused patches are easier to review.

3. **Documentation matters more than you think:**  
   A great doc update has been merged faster for me than a clever-but-confusing code patch.

4. **Ask before you build:**  
   Other’s time is precious—align your plan with the repo’s direction before investing hours.

5. **Rejection is a Redirection:**  
   Every “not now” taught me something.  
   I started seeing PR feedback as a (free!) code review, not a personal attack.

---

## How I Got Over The Sting

- I stopped refreshing for approval. I got moving on the next thing.
- I started viewing my PR as part of the project’s story, not just my story.
- Now I thank maintainers—even when they say _no_.

---

## What I Would Tell Any New Contributor

Feedback is a gift. If your PR is rejected, you’re not a failure, you’re a **work in progress** (join the club).  
Take a break, rewrite, and hit “Create PR” again.
